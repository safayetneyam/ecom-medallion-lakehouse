### What these numbers mean for Silver

* **Quarantine candidates:** rows with a hard rule failure (null mandatory field, unparseable or out-of-range number, invalid/future timestamp, unknown status/currency, orphan/null order reference).
* **Standardise & accept:** `inconsistent_case_whitespace` rows are valid once you `trim()` + normalise case; they should *not* be quarantined. That's a test that your rules clean before they reject.
* **Duplicate losers:** `exact_duplicate_row` (whole row repeated) and `duplicate_*_id_diff_content` (same business key, different status/quantity/timestamp, like a re-extracted update). Each injected duplicate pairs with one clean original, so expect exactly one loser per injected row.
* **Derived orphans (not injected):** some otherwise-valid transactions point at an order whose row is quarantined in Silver. Whether those count as orphans depends on whether you check the relationship against Bronze or against accepted `silver_orders`. Document your choice.

## Reference values for Silver rules

* Order statuses: `delivered`, `shipped`, `processing`, `pending`, `cancelled`, `returned`
* Categories: `Electronics`, `Fashion`, `Home & Kitchen`, `Beauty`, `Sports`, `Books`, `Toys`, `Grocery`
* Countries: `Bangladesh`, `India`, `United States`, `United Kingdom`, `Germany`, `France`, `Canada`, `Australia`, `Singapore`, `United Arab Emirates`
* Payment methods: `credit_card`, `debit_card`, `mobile_wallet`, `paypal`, `bank_transfer`, `cash_on_delivery`
* Transaction statuses: `SUCCESS`, `FAILED`, `PENDING`, `REFUNDED`
* Supported currencies: `USD`, `EUR`, `GBP`, `BDT`, `INR`, `CAD`, `AUD`, `SGD`, `AED` (see `fx_rates.csv`; static rates, USD per unit)
* `quantity` between 1 and 50; `unit_price` between 0.5 and 10,000.0 (USD); transaction amount converted to USD must not exceed 500,000
* Null-like tokens to treat as NULL after `trim()`: `''`, `NULL`, `null`, `None`, `N/A`, `n/a`
* Date format `yyyy-MM-dd` (order_date); timestamp format `yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm:ss` (txn_ts)

## Why the anomalies stay a small, measured fraction

About 9.2% of order rows and 8.9% of transaction rows are anomalous. Of that, 3 points are cosmetic case and whitespace issues that Silver should fix rather than reject, and every other class sits between 0.2% and 1%. That keeps the dataset representative, so the Gold metrics still describe a believable business, while giving each rule enough hits to be visible in the reconciliation. Because every count is known in advance, each Silver rule can be checked exactly (expected rows equal quarantined rows). If the dirty share were large or unknown, you couldn't tell a broken rule from bad data.
